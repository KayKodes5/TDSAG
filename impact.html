Who we are

A registered non-profit, since 1986.

TDSAG works to improve the quality of life of all people born with Down syndrome and other intellectual disabilities, to promote their right to live with independence and dignity, and to be active members of their communities. We align our services with international trends and best practice in intellectual disability.

We respect religious and cultural diversity, and prioritise our work in disadvantaged communities. Over 75% of the people we help are poor and vulnerable to ongoing discrimination and marginalisation.

What we stand for

Our social mandate

Alone we can do so little – together we can achieve so much.

Independence and dignity

We promote the right of every person with an intellectual disability to make choices and live a dignified, independent life.

Active members of society

Given the right opportunities and support, people with Down syndrome are active, valued members of their communities.

Reaching those most in need

We respect every culture and faith, and focus our work where support is hardest to find: in disadvantaged communities.

Our mission

What we commit to, for people with Down syndrome and other intellectual disabilities and their families.

  1. Provide community services that help people integrate into society, gain equal opportunities, and receive the support and care they need.
  2. Offer community support for parents, families and caregivers.
  3. Create opportunities that help people function well and reach their potential.
  4. Develop our policies and programmes with input from people with Down syndrome and their families.
  5. Work with organisations, local, provincial and national government, and individuals to promote their interests and public awareness.
  6. Support research on Down syndrome and other intellectual disabilities.

What we do

Free support, from birth throughout adulthood.

We provide free outreach support groups, early intervention and self-advocacy training programmes to people and their families living with Down syndrome and other intellectual disabilities. We also run training workshops for families, educators, therapists and community leaders, to build local support networks.

Our journey

Nearly four decades of great strides.

Since 1986 we have worked to change how people with Down syndrome are seen and supported in Gauteng, and to make sure their rights are respected.

Our awareness work has helped change public perceptions of Down syndrome, and our support and intervention programmes have transformed lives.

[Add key dated milestones since 1986.]

We work in the spirit of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, which South Africa ratified in 2007. That commits the country to removing barriers to inclusion, including effective early intervention and employment opportunities.

The Constitution strives to build an inclusive mindset towards all vulnerable people, and the Promotion of Equality and Prevention of Unfair Discrimination Act and the Employment Equity Act reinforce equal opportunities for people with disabilities. Our programmes also align with the White Paper on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2015).

We work alongside local government, public and private care professionals, academic institutions including Schools of Specialised Education, and international intellectual disability organisations.

In South Africa, Down syndrome is still culturally stigmatised. Some parents are led to believe their child’s disability is their fault, and children miss out on education and health care. Early support changes that.
